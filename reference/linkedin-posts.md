The path to becoming an Aircraft Maintenance Engineer in Canada is straightforward — but most people don't know where to start.

Here's the 5-step roadmap:

1️⃣ Meet the education requirement
Complete a Transport Canada-approved AME program (BCIT, NAIT, Sault College, etc.) or document 48 months of relevant work experience.

2️⃣ Get your Student Pilot Permit or equivalent
You'll need TCCA medical clearance before writing exams.

3️⃣ Pass Transport Canada written exams
Five exams covering Airframe, Powerplant, Structures, Electrical, and CARs. Pass mark: 70%.

4️⃣ Log practical experience
Two years (M1/M2) or three years (E/S) of hands-on maintenance under supervision.

5️⃣ Write the oral/practical exam
The final hurdle with a Transport Canada inspector.

Most aspiring AMEs stall at step 3 — the written exams. The syllabus is broad, the questions are specific, and PDFs don't prepare you for the actual exam format.

## Post 5 — Comparison / Expert Opinion: "M1 vs M2 vs E vs S — which AME licence is right for you?"

**Format:** Comparison breakdown
**Target:** Prospective students, career advisors, high school STEM
**Best time:** Monday 9:00 AM ET

---

One of the most common questions I get: *What's the difference between M1 and M2? Should I go for an E licence instead?*

Here's a simple breakdown:

🛩️ **M1 (Aircraft Maintenance Engineer — Small Aircraft)**
- Works on aircraft under 5,700 kg MTOW
- Piston and turboprop singles/twins
- Quicker path to licensing (2 years experience)
- Great for GA shops, flight schools, bush operators

🛩️ **M2 (Aircraft Maintenance Engineer — Large Aircraft)**
- Works on aircraft over 5,700 kg MTOW
- Jets, airliners, heavies (Air Canada, WestJet cargo, etc.)
- Requires 2 years experience
- Highest earning potential in the category

⚡ **E (Aviation Maintenance Technician — Electronics)**
- Avionics, instruments, electrical systems, autopilots
- 3 years experience requirement
- High demand in both GA and airline settings
- Best for those who love troubleshooting electronics

🛠️ **S (Aviation Maintenance Technician — Structures)**
- Sheet metal, composites, welding, painting
- 3 years experience requirement
- Essential in MROs and airline heavy-check bays

Which one is "best"? There's no single answer — it depends on what kind of work you want to do and where you want your career to go.

My advice: pick the work you'd enjoy doing every day. All four paths lead to solid careers with great pay.
